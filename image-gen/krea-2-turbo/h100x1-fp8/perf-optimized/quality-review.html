Grading key (each grade describes the image's difference from the bf16 baseline at the same prompt and seed):
  • reference — the bf16 baseline output itself; the ground truth the other columns are compared against.
  • negligible — pixel-level differences exist (hashes differ) but you need side-by-side inspection to find them. Composition, subject, style, text legibility, and anatomy all preserved. Safe to treat as "same quality".
  • near-lossless — same bar as negligible, used for configs whose only change is numerical kernel drift (e.g. torch.compile): same-seed output differs only in fine low-signal detail.
  • visible — a difference you can spot at a glance: a color shift, softened fine detail in dense scenes, or a composition divergence (same seed produces a different but equally good layout; the denoise trajectory shifted, quality did not drop). The image stays coherent and artifact-free. These are exactly the cases your judgment is needed on.
  • unacceptable — genuine degradation: artifacts, faceted gradients, mushy line art, damaged text or anatomy. Disqualifies the configuration as a default.
  • not yet generated — that variant's panel has not been produced; the column renders dimmed.
